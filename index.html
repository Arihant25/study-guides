How to use this page

There are 12 proofs. Half of them (the BA family) share one method, so it is less than it looks.

  1. Learn the formulas in the next section until you can write each one with its "where" line.
  2. Start with BA (Part 3). Learn the rate dkidt, then P(k) by continuum theory. Models A and B and the nonlinear case reuse that method with a different Π. You write dkidt=mΠ and integrate from ki(ti)=m, and where P(k) is needed, the uniform joining times give it.
  3. Then the master equation. It is the longest proof, and the board names it, so write it out at least twice.
  4. Next, the small world (Part 4). Learn C and ℓ‾ at p=0 and p=1, then (1−p)3.
  5. Synchronisation and Lorenz (Parts 1 and 2) need one write-through each, since you learned them for Quiz 2.
  6. Finally, do the past questions with the page closed, then the flashcards.

For every proof, learn the shaded one-line idea first, then the steps. The 2026 mid-sem gave 0 for a formula with no derivation, so always write the steps, even when you remember the answer.

Formulas to remember

Know these by heart. Under each one, "where" says what every letter means. Every result is proved further down.

Symbols used everywhere
  • x˙ (x dot)How fast x changes with time, dx/dt. Two dots, x¨, is how fast x˙ changes (acceleration).
  • x∗ (x star)A fixed point: a value where nothing changes any more (x˙=0, or xn+1=xn for a map).
  • f′(x)The slope (derivative) of f at x. In x˙=f(x), f is the rule that gives the rate of change.
  • ∂f/∂xPartial derivative: the slope of f as x changes, holding the other variables fixed.
  • λ (lambda)An eigenvalue. Near a fixed point, a small push grows or shrinks like eλt, so negative means it dies out (stable).
  • i−1. Also eiθ=cos⁡θ+isin⁡θ, a point at angle θ on the unit circle.
  • ⟨ ⟩"The average of". ⟨k⟩ is the average degree, ⟨k2⟩ the average of the squared degrees.
  • iff"If and only if": the condition works both ways.

1 · Phase synchronisation

  • The modelθ˙1=ω1+k1sin⁡(θ2−θ1), θ˙2=ω2+k2sin⁡(θ1−θ2)where θ1,2 are the phases (angles) of the two oscillators. Each ω is a natural frequency, how fast that oscillator turns on its own, and each k says how strongly it is pulled towards the other.
  • Phase differenceϕ˙=Δω−(k1+k2)sin⁡ϕwhere ϕ=θ2−θ1 and Δω=ω2−ω1.
  • Critical conditionLock iff |Δω|≤k1+k2. Equal couplings k: kc=|Δω|2where locking means ϕ stops changing, so both turn at one common frequency.
  • Common frequencyΩ=k2ω1+k1ω2k1+k2a weighted average of the two natural frequencies. With k1=k2 it is the plain average.

2 · The Lorenz system

  • The modelx˙=σ(y−x), y˙=ρx−y−xz, z˙=xy−βzwhere all the constants are positive.
  • Fixed points(0,0,0), and for ρ>1: (±β(ρ−1), ±β(ρ−1), ρ−1)where the two signs are taken together.
  • JacobianJ=(−σσ0ρ−z−1−xyx−β)where row i holds the slopes of the i-th rate, taken with respect to each variable in turn. Put each fixed point into it.
  • OriginStable iff ρ<1because J(0) splits into −β and a 2×2 block with trace −(σ+1)<0 and determinant σ(1−ρ).

3 · Scale-free networks

  • BA modelGrowth + preferential attachment Π(ki)=ki∑jkjwhere each step adds one node with m links, and ki is the degree of node i.
  • Degree of one nodedkidt=ki2t, ki(t)=m(tti)1/2where t is the time now (= number of nodes added) and ti the time node i joined, with ki(ti)=m.
  • Degree distributionP(k)=2m2k−3, or exactly 2m(m+1)k(k+1)(k+2) from the master equationwhere P(k) is the fraction of nodes with degree k. Exponent γ=3 for every m, and it does not change as the network grows.
  • Master equation(N+1)P(k,t+1)=NP(k,t)+k−12P(k−1,t)−k2P(k,t)for k>m. The equation at k=m is (N+1)P(m,t+1)=NP(m,t)+1−m2P(m,t). Here P(k,t) is the fraction of degree-k nodes at time t, and N=t.
  • No preferential attachmentki=m+mln⁡tti, P(k)=eme−k/mModel A keeps growth, but each new link picks a node uniformly. The result is an exponential, so there are no hubs.
  • No growthki(t)≈2tN for every nodeModel B fixes N and adds one link per step. All degrees become about equal, and after about N2/2 steps the graph is complete.
  • Nonlinear attachmentΠ(ki)=kiα∑jkjα. Below α=1 hubs stay small, and above it one hub takes almost every linkwhere α≥0, and α=1 is the BA model. The largest hub grows like (ln⁡t)1/(1−α) below 1 and like t above 1, against t1/2 for BA.

4 · Small-world graphs

  • ClusteringCi=links among the neighbours of i(ki2), C=1n∑iCiwhere (ki2)=ki(ki−1)2 is the number of pairs of neighbours.
  • Path lengthℓ‾=1n(n−1)∑u≠vℓ(u,v)where ℓ(u,v) is the number of steps on a shortest path from u to v.
  • p=0, ring latticeC(0)=3(k−1)2(2k−1)→34, ℓ‾(0)≈n4kwhere each of the n nodes links to k neighbours on each side, so the degree is 2k.
  • p=1, random graphC≈2kn, ℓ‾≈ln⁡nln⁡2ksame n and the same degree 2k.
  • In betweenC(p)=C(0)(1−p)3where p is the rewiring probability. For small p, ℓ‾ is already near the random value while C is still near C(0). That range of p is the small world.

1 · Phase synchronisation

Two oscillators each turn at their own speed. Coupling pulls each towards the other. If the pull is strong enough compared with the difference in speeds, they lock and turn together. The board's f1(ω1,θ1,θ2,k1) is ω1+k1sin⁡(θ2−θ1).

Two coupled oscillators: find when they lock and the common frequency Ω.

Subtract to get one equation for the phase difference. Locking means that difference stops changing, and a sine can't exceed 1.

  • θ˙1=ω1+k1sin⁡(θ2−θ1), θ˙2=ω2+k2sin⁡(θ1−θ2)
  • ϕ˙=(ω2−ω1)−(k1+k2)sin⁡ϕϕ=θ2−θ1, subtract
  • sin⁡ϕ∗=ω2−ω1k1+k2locked means ϕ˙=0
  • Lock iff |ω2−ω1|≤k1+k2|sin⁡|≤1
  • Stable root has cos⁡ϕ∗>0slope −(k1+k2)cos⁡ϕ must be negative
  • Ω=k2ω1+k1ω2k1+k2Ω=ω1+k1sin⁡ϕ∗=ω2−k2sin⁡ϕ∗. Weight and add.

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §15

Numbers

Take ω1=2 and ω2=10. With k1=k2=2 the coupling adds up to only 4, which is less than the gap of 8, so they never lock. They lock once k1+k2≥8, so with equal couplings the critical value is kc=4. At k1=k2=5 the locked state has sin⁡ϕ∗=810, so ϕ∗=0.93 rad (the stable root, since cos⁡ϕ∗>0), and the common frequency is Ω=5⋅2+5⋅1010=6.

The After mid-sem guide, §15, has a longer version with the phase-line picture.

2 · The Lorenz system

There are three equations in three unknowns. Solve for the fixed points from the easy equations first, then write one general Jacobian and put each point into it.

Lorenz system: find the fixed points and show that the origin is stable iff ρ<1.

Solve the three equations from the easy ones first. At the origin the z part separates, leaving a 2×2 block.

  • y=xσ(y−x)=0
  • z=x2/βxy−βz=0
  • x(ρ−1−z)=0, so P0=(0,0,0) or z=ρ−1, x=y=±β(ρ−1)real only for ρ>1
  • J=(−σσ0ρ−z−1−xyx−β)
  • At P0: λ=−β, and λ2+(σ+1)λ+σ(1−ρ)=0
  • Both roots negative iff σ(1−ρ)>0, i.e. ρ<1trace already negative, need positive determinant
  • At P1,2, with c=β(ρ−1): J=(−σσ01−1∓c±c±c−β)put x=y=±c, z=ρ−1, so ρ−z=1

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §14

3 · Scale-free networks and the Barabási–Albert model

Real networks grow, and new nodes prefer well-connected nodes. Those two ingredients give a power law with exponent 3. The board asks for the rate dkidt and the distribution P(k), with the master equation as a second way to get P(k). It then asks what happens when you remove an ingredient or change one.

Barabási–Albert model: show dkidt=ki2t and solve it.

Each new node brings m links, and each picks node i with probability ki over the total degree. The total degree is 2mt. Separate and integrate from ki(ti)=m.

  • Growth adds one node with m links per step, and preferential attachment gives Π(ki)=ki/∑jkj
  • dkidt=mΠ(ki)=mki∑j=1N−1kjm chances per step, ki treated as smooth
  • ∑j=1N−1kj=2mt−mmt links each add 2, minus the new node's m
  • dkidt=ki2t−1≈ki2t
  • dkiki=12dtt, so ln⁡kim=12ln⁡ttinode i joins at ti with m links
  • ki(t)=m(t/ti)1/2, and dkidt=m2titolder nodes gain faster, and everyone slows down

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §20

Barabási–Albert model: derive P(k)=2m2k−3 (continuum theory).

Degree is fixed by joining time, and joining times are uniform. A node has degree below k exactly when it joined late.

  • ki=m(t/ti)1/2⇒ti=m2t/ki2
  • P(ki<k)=P(ti>m2t/k2)a larger degree means an earlier joining time
  • P(ti>T)=1−T/tone node per step, so ti is uniform on 1…t
  • P(ki<k)=1−m2/k2t cancels, so the distribution doesn't change as the network grows
  • P(k)=ddkP(ki<k)=2m2k−3, so γ=3 for every m

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §21

Barabási–Albert model: write the master (rate) equation and solve it for P(k).

Count the degree-k nodes before and after one step. They are gained from degree k−1 and lost to degree k+1, except at k=m, where the new node itself is the gain. Then assume a steady state and climb up from m.

  • Π(k)=k2mt, so the links landing on degree-k nodes in one step number k2mt⋅NP(k,t)⋅m=k2P(k,t)N=t
  • (N+1)P(k,t+1)=NP(k,t)+k−12P(k−1,t)−k2P(k,t) for k>m
  • (N+1)P(m,t+1)=NP(m,t)+1−m2P(m,t)the new node arrives with degree m
  • In the steady state the left side minus NP is P(k), so P(k)=k−1k+2P(k−1) and P(m)=2m+2
  • P(m+1)=2m(m+2)(m+3), P(m+2)=2m(m+1)(m+2)(m+3)(m+4), …
  • P(k)=2m(m+1)k(k+1)(k+2)∼2m2k−3as a check, k=m gives 2m+2

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §21

3a · Growth without preferential attachment (Model A)

Keep growth, but let each new link pick an existing node uniformly at random, whatever its degree. There are m0+t−1≈t nodes to choose from, so

Π(ki)=1m0+t−1≈1t,dkidt=mΠ=mt.

The rate no longer depends on ki, so a big node gains links no faster than a small one. Integrating from ki(ti)=m gives a logarithm instead of a square root, ki(t)=m+mln⁡(t/ti). Old nodes still have more links, but only logarithmically more. The same "late joiners have small degree" argument as for BA then gives an exponential:

P(k)=eme−k/m(k≥m)

It adds up to 1, since ∫m∞1me1−k/mdk=e⋅e−1=1.

Growth but no preferential attachment (Model A): show the degree distribution is exponential.

Same steps as the BA model, but every existing node is equally likely, so the rate is m/t instead of ki/2t. That gives a logarithm instead of a square root, and an exponential instead of a power law.

  • Π(ki)=1m0+t−1≈1tuniform choice among the ≈t nodes
  • dkidt=mΠ=mtdoes not depend on ki
  • ki(t)=m+mln⁡tti=mln⁡(etti)integrate from ki(ti)=m
  • ki<k⟺ti>te1−k/m
  • P(ki<k)=1−e1−k/mti is uniform, so P(ti>T)=1−T/t
  • P(k)=eme−k/m for k≥mdifferentiate, and there are no hubs

3b · Preferential attachment without growth (Model B)

Now keep preferential attachment but fix the number of nodes at N. Nothing is ever added except links. At each step one node chosen at random gets a new link, and preferential attachment chooses the other end. After t steps there are t links, so ∑jkj=2t, and node i gains a link either by being the random end (probability 1N) or by being picked (probability ki2t):

dkidt=1N+ki2t.

Trying ki=at gives a=1N+a2, so a=2N. Without the 1N the equation is the BA one, which t solves. So the full solution is

ki(t)=2tN+Cit≈2tN  for large t.

The part that differs between nodes, Cit, becomes negligible next to 2tN, which is the same for everyone. So all degrees end up about equal, and the distribution is a narrow peak around the average instead of a power law. Since there are only N(N−1)2 possible links, after about N22 steps every pair is linked and the network is a complete graph. There is no stationary state.

Preferential attachment but no growth (Model B): show every node ends up with the same degree.

Fix N nodes. Each step a random node links to a node picked by preferential attachment. The random end gives every node the same 1/N per step, and that term wins.

  • Each step adds one link, so ∑jkj=2t
  • dkidt=1N+ki2tchosen as the random end, or picked by Π(ki)=ki/2t
  • Trying ki=at gives a=1N+a2, so a=2N
  • ki(t)=2tN+Citt solves the equation without the 1N
  • For large t the 2tN part wins, so all degrees become about equal and the distribution is a narrow peak instead of a power law
  • Complete graph after about N2/2 stepsN(N−1)/2 possible links

Explained: End-sem sheet

What to remember

Both ingredients are needed. Growth alone gives an exponential. Preferential attachment alone gives a network where everyone ends up alike and then complete. Only together do they give P(k)∼k−3.

3c · Nonlinear preferential attachment

Keep growth, and change the rule to a power of the degree:

Π(ki)=kiα∑jkjα,α≥0.

α=0 is Model A (every node equal) and α=1 is the BA model. The question is what happens in between and beyond.

αNameDegree distributionLargest hub after t steps
0no preferential attachmentexponential∼ln⁡t
0<α<1sublineara stretched exponential, about e−ck1−α, so no power law and few hubs∼(ln⁡t)1/(1−α)
1linear (BA)power law k−3∼t1/2
α>1superlinearwinner takes all, with one hub and the rest small∼t

Why α<1 gives small hubs. When the degrees stay small, kjα has a finite average ν=⟨kα⟩, so the sum over the ≈t nodes is ∑jkjα≈νt. Then

dkidt=mkiανt⇒∫mkik−αdk=mν∫titdt′t′⇒ki1−α−m1−α=(1−α)mνln⁡tti.

For the oldest node (ti=1) this gives kmax⁡∼(ln⁡t)1/(1−α), which grows more slowly than any power of t. Putting α=0 and ν=1 gives back Model A's ki=m+mln⁡(t/ti), which is a useful check.

Why α>1 gives one giant hub. Suppose one node has a degree that grows like t, say k1=ct, while the others stay small. Then k1α=cαtα, and since α>1 this outgrows the sum over all the other nodes, which is only about t. So Π(1)→1. Almost every new node links to node 1, which keeps k1 growing like t. The guess is self-consistent. One node, usually one of the first, ends up linked to a finite fraction of the network.

Nonlinear preferential attachment Π(ki)=kiα/∑jkjα. How do the hubs change with α?

For α<1 the sum in the bottom grows like t, as in Model A, so hubs grow only like a power of ln⁡t. For α>1 the biggest node dominates the sum and takes almost every new link.

  • α=0 gives Model A (exponential), and α=1 gives BA, with P(k)∼k−3 and kmax⁡∼t1/2
  • For α<1, ∑jkjα≈νt with ν=⟨kα⟩ finitedegrees stay small, N≈t
  • dkidt=mkiανt⇒ki1−α−m1−α=(1−α)mνln⁡tti
  • kmax⁡∼(ln⁡t)1/(1−α), P(k) a stretched exponential, roughly e−ck1−αoldest node, ti=1
  • For α>1, if one node has k1∝t, then k1α≫t≥ all the rest, so Π(1)→1self-consistent
  • Winner takes all. One hub holds a finite fraction of the links (kmax⁡∼t), while the others stay small

4 · Small-world graphs

Start from a ring lattice (p=0), which has high clustering and long paths. Rewire each link with probability p. At p=1 the graph is random, with short paths and almost no clustering. In between, a few rewired links make the paths short while the clustering stays high. The board asks for the average clustering coefficient and the path length at both ends.

Convention

Here k is the number of neighbours on each side, so the degree is 2k. If a question writes K for the degree, put k=K/2. Then C(0)=3(K−2)4(K−1) and ℓ‾(0)≈n2K.

Ring lattice (k neighbours on each side): show C(0)=3(k−1)2(2k−1).

Count the pairs of neighbours, then the pairs that are linked, which are the ones at most k positions apart. Two neighbours on the same side always are, and two on opposite sides are when their offsets add up to at most k.

  • There are (2k2)=k(2k−1) possible pairs2k neighbours, degree 2k
  • Pairs on the same side number 2(k2)any two on one side are within k
  • Pairs across i, at offsets −a and +b with a+b≤k, number (k−1)+(k−2)+⋯+0=(k2)
  • Linked pairs =3(k2)=32k(k−1)
  • C(0)=32k(k−1)k(2k−1)=3(k−1)2(2k−1)→34does not depend on n

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §25

Ring lattice: show the average path length is ℓ‾(0)≈n4k.

Every step reaches exactly 2k new nodes (flat shells), so distances run evenly from 1 to the farthest, and the average is half the farthest.

  • Shell ℓ holds 2k nodes for every ℓk more on each side
  • The farthest node is n/2 positions away and each step covers k, so it takes D=n2k steps
  • ℓ‾(0)=1n∑ℓ=1D2kℓ≈2kn⋅D22=n4k
  • ℓ‾∝n, so this is a large world

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §25

Random graph with the same degree 2k (Watts–Strogatz at p=1): find C and ℓ‾.

Edges are independent, so a pair of neighbours is linked with the same probability p as any pair. Shells multiply by 2k, so the network is used up after a logarithmic number of steps.

  • p=2kn−1≈2knmean degree p(n−1)=2k
  • C=p(ki2)(ki2)=p=2kn→0
  • The shells hold 1,2k,(2k)2,… nodesno clustering, so the tree argument holds
  • (2k)ℓ‾≈n⇒ℓ‾≈ln⁡nln⁡2k

Full proof: After mid-sem guide §25

Watts–Strogatz model: show C(p)=C(0)(1−p)3.

A triangle survives only if none of its three links was rewired. Each survives with probability 1−p, independently.

  • Rewire each of the nk lattice links with probability p, and the mean degree stays 2k
  • P(triangle survives) =(1−p)3three independent links
  • A rewired link makes a new triangle only with probability O(1/n), which is neglected
  • C(p)=C(0)(1−p)3=3(k−1)2(2k−1)(1−p)3≈C(0)(1−3p) for small p
  • There are about nkp shortcuts, the first at p≈1nk, so ℓ‾ falls long before C

Mid-sem · Q1 · 3 + 4 marksTwo coupled phase oscillators: θ˙1=ω1+k1sin⁡(θ2−θ1), θ˙2=ω2+k2sin⁡(θ1−θ2). (a) Calculate the critical coupling strength at which both oscillators follow the common frequency. (b) Calculate the common frequency. Can one obtain a common (and stable) frequency at k1=k2=k=2, ω1=2, ω2=10?›

The idea in plain words. The oscillators share a frequency when the gap between their angles stops changing. The gap changes because of the difference in natural speeds, and the coupling pulls against it, but the pull is at most k1+k2 (a sine is never more than 1). So they can lock only if the coupling is at least as big as the speed difference.

Formulas you need

  • ϕ=θ2−θ1, ϕ˙=(ω2−ω1)−(k1+k2)sin⁡ϕ (§15).
  • |sin⁡ϕ|≤1.

Step by step

  1. Subtract the equations to get ϕ˙ above.
  2. Locked means ϕ˙=0, so sin⁡ϕ∗=ω2−ω1k1+k2. This needs |ω2−ω1|≤k1+k2, and the critical coupling is k1+k2=|ω2−ω1|.
  3. The lock is stable when the slope −(k1+k2)cos⁡ϕ∗ is negative, that is when cos⁡ϕ∗>0.
  4. For the common frequency, write Ω=ω1+k1sin⁡ϕ∗=ω2−k2sin⁡ϕ∗ and eliminate sin⁡ϕ∗. That gives Ω=k2ω1+k1ω2k1+k2.
  5. Numbers. |ω2−ω1|=8, k1+k2=4. sin⁡ϕ∗=2 is impossible. No lock.

Final answer

(a) They lock iff k1+k2≥|ω2−ω1|, so the critical coupling is k1+k2=|ω2−ω1|, or kc=|ω2−ω1|/2 when the couplings are equal. (b) Ω=k2ω1+k1ω2k1+k2. With k=2 the coupling adds up to only 4 against a gap of ω2−ω1=8, so there is no common stable frequency. Each k would need to be at least 4. (If they locked, Ω would be 6.)

Quiz 2 · Set 2 · Q4 · 4 marksFind the fixed points of the Lorenz oscillator x˙=σ(y−x), y˙=ρx−y−xz, z˙=−βz+xy. Construct the associated Jacobians. (Eigenvalue analysis is not required.)›

The idea in plain words. Set all three rates to zero and solve one equation at a time. The first says y=x, and the third then gives z in terms of x. After that, the middle one either forces x=0 or fixes z=ρ−1. The Jacobian is the table of partial derivatives, evaluated at each fixed point.

Formulas you need

  • At a fixed point all rates are zero. The Jacobian is Jij=∂fi/∂xj (§5, §14).

Step by step

  1. σ(y−x)=0⇒y=x.
  2. xy−βz=0⇒z=x2/β.
  3. ρx−x−xz=x(ρ−1−z)=0⇒x=0 or z=ρ−1.
  4. If x=0, the point is P0=(0,0,0). If z=ρ−1, then x2=β(ρ−1), which gives P1,2=(±β(ρ−1),±β(ρ−1),ρ−1). These exist only for ρ>1.
  5. The general Jacobian is J=(−σσ0ρ−z−1−xyx−β).
  6. J(P0)=(−σσ0ρ−1000−β). J(P1,2)=(−σσ01−1∓c±c±c−β) with c=β(ρ−1).
  7. Numbers. For the usual values (σ=10, β=8/3, ρ=28), c=72=8.49, P1,2=(±8.49,±8.49,27), J(P1)=(−101001−1−8.498.498.49−2.67).

Final answer

P0=(0,0,0) and, for ρ>1, P1,2=(±β(ρ−1),±β(ρ−1),ρ−1), with the Jacobians above.

Quiz 2 · Set 2 · Q1 · 2 + 2 marksExplain the basic ingredients required for generating a BA graph. Prove that the rate at which an existing node i acquires links, dkidt, as a result of new nodes connecting to it is proportional to kit, where k is the degree and t is time.›

The idea in plain words. A BA network grows one node at a time, and each new node prefers to link to nodes that already have many links. A node's chance of getting each new link is its share of all the link ends in the network. That total grows in proportion to time, so the rate of gaining links is the node's degree divided by time.

Formulas you need

  • Π(ki)=ki∑jkj, and ∑jkj=2×(number of links) (§19, §20).

Part 1 · the ingredients

  1. Growth. Start from m0 nodes. At each time step add one new node with m≤m0 links to nodes already present. The number of nodes keeps increasing, unlike in a random graph where N is fixed.
  2. Preferential attachment. The probability that a link of the new node goes to node i is proportional to its degree, Π(ki)=ki/∑jkj. Unlike in a random graph, partners are not chosen with equal probability.
  3. Together they give a scale-free network with P(k)∼k−3. Either one alone does not.

Part 2 · the rate

  1. Treat ki as a continuous quantity (its average over many runs).
  2. Each step brings m new links, and each chooses node i with probability Π(ki), so dkidt=mΠ(ki)=mki∑j=1N−1kj.
  3. After t steps there are mt links, so the total degree is 2mt. Leaving out the new node's own m gives ∑j=1N−1kj=2mt−m.
  4. So dkidt=mki2mt−m=ki2t−1≈ki2tfor large t. The rate is proportional to ki/t, with constant 12.

Final answer

The two ingredients are growth and preferential attachment, and dkidt=ki2t∝kit. If there is time, add the solution ki(t)=m(t/ti)1/2, which gives the same rate as dkidt=m2tit∝t−1/2.

The scan of this paper is hard to read at "proportional to". The reading ki/t is the one that fits the slides' derivation.

Practice · 2 + 3 marksA network grows by one node with m links at each step, and each link chooses an existing node uniformly at random. (a) Find dkidt and ki(t). (b) Find P(k). Is the network scale-free?›
  1. Π=1m0+t−1≈1t, so dkidt=mt.
  2. Integrate from ki(ti)=m: ki(t)=m+mln⁡tti.
  3. ki<k⟺ti>te1−k/m. Joining times are uniform, so P(ki<k)=1−e1−k/m.
  4. Differentiating gives P(k)=eme−k/m.

The result is an exponential, not a power law, so the network is not scale-free. Without preferential attachment there are no hubs.

Practice · 3 + 2 marksWrite the master equation for the number of degree-k nodes in the BA model, including the equation for k=m. Solve it in the steady state and show P(k)∼k−3.›

Follow the master-equation proof. Each of these steps earns marks.

  1. Π(k)=k2mt, so the links landing on degree-k nodes in one step number k2P(k,t).
  2. Degree-k nodes are gained from degree k−1 and lost to degree k+1.
  3. At k=m the new node adds an extra +1.
  4. In the steady state, P(k)=k−1k+2P(k−1) and P(m)=2m+2.
  5. Multiplying out gives P(k)=2m(m+1)k(k+1)(k+2), which is ≈2m2k−3 for large k.
Practice · 2 + 2 marksWhat happens to the BA model if (a) the number of nodes is fixed, (b) the attachment probability is Π∝kα with α>1?›

(a) dkidt=1N+ki2t, solved by ki=2tN+Cit. The common 2tN part wins, so all degrees become about equal (a narrow peak, no power law), and after about N2/2 steps the graph is complete.

(b) Winner takes all. If one node has k1∝t, then k1α grows faster than the sum over all the others (about t), so it receives almost every new link. One hub ends up with a finite fraction of the links, kmax⁡∼t.
